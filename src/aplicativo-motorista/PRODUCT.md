# Product

## Register

product

## Users

VanRides serves four operational roles:

- Drivers operating a planned van route, often while managing timing, passenger stops, location sharing and route progress.
- Passengers who need to understand where the van is, whether their trip is active, how many stops remain and what they should do next.
- Guardians or responsible adults who track linked passengers, presence decisions and trip progress with a high need for trust.
- Company admins who plan services, trips, routes, passengers, drivers and operational exceptions.

The mobile experience is the primary product surface for live operations. The admin portal supports the operation, but the emotional center of the product is the live map experience for driver, passenger and guardian.

## Product Purpose

VanRides is an Uber-like operational experience for vans. It does not match riders with drivers on demand; it coordinates planned van trips with routes, stops, passengers, guardians and company oversight.

The product should make a shared route feel as clear and trustworthy as an individual ride. A user should quickly understand where the van is, what is happening now, what happens next, how many stops remain and whether the system is up to date.

Success means users trust the app during the trip without needing to call or message for basic status. The live map, ETA, stop count and route progress should answer the core question: "where is the van, and what does that mean for me?"

## Brand Personality

VanRides should feel:

- Reliable: current state, location and next steps are obvious and credible.
- Premium: the app feels mature, polished and intentional, not like a prototype or a generic map wrapper.
- Useful: every visible element helps a user make a practical decision.

The primary reference is Uber, especially its sense of a living map, clear trip state, confident location feedback and minimal cognitive load. VanRides adapts that reference to shared van operations: multiple passengers, planned routes, stop order, guardians and company-managed service days.

## Anti-references

VanRides should not look or feel like:

- Google Maps pasted into the app.
- A dashboard full of cards competing with the map.
- A childish school transportation app.
- A generic fleet tracker.

These anti-references are especially important in the map-first experience. The map is not a background widget; it is the operational stage where the van, passenger, route, stop count and ETA become understandable.

## Design Principles

1. Make the map feel alive, not decorative.
   The van, passenger, route line, next stop and progress should communicate status before the user reads a card.

2. Prioritize the next useful fact.
   Passenger and guardian surfaces should answer location, ETA and stops remaining before secondary details. Driver surfaces should answer next stop, action and route progress before history or admin context.

3. Use shared-route clarity.
   VanRides is not a one-passenger ride. The interface must make stop order, passenger position in the queue and remaining stops feel natural.

4. Keep premium restraint.
   Premium means confident hierarchy, polished motion, custom markers, good spacing and crisp language. It does not mean visual noise, heavy gradients or decorative complexity.

5. Design for operational confidence.
   Empty, stale, delayed, no-signal and offline states must be honest and calming. The user should know whether the app is waiting, tracking, stale or blocked.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

VanRides targets WCAG AA as the default accessibility bar.

The product should support strong contrast on map overlays and sheets, dynamic text where feasible, simple operational language, touch targets suitable for mobile use and reduced-motion alternatives for animated map transitions or vehicle movement.

Color must not be the only way to communicate trip state, stop status, alert severity or route progress. Motion should reinforce state changes without becoming required for comprehension.
